\section{1. Introduction and game rules}

Modified Wallz is a two-player, Quoridor-like pawn race on an N x N grid. Player 0 starts at
\texttt{(0, N//2)} and must reach row N-1; player 1 starts at \texttt{(N-1, N//2)} and must reach row 0.
On each turn a player makes one action. \texttt{wallz\_engine.py} is the only source of the rules
(\texttt{docs/game\_rules.md}, frozen in \texttt{docs/reports/stage1\_engine\_freeze.md}).

\begin{center}\scriptsize
\begin{longtable}{p{0.45\linewidth}p{0.45\linewidth}}
\toprule
Action & Rule \\
\midrule
MOVE & one orthogonal step; not across a wall, onto the opponent, onto a blocked cell, or off the board; no pawn jump \\
WALL & a 2-cell wall; costs 1 of the \texttt{M} walls; becomes the player's latest wall \\
REPLACE (A) & moves only the player's latest wall; costs nothing; allowed with 0 walls left \\
HOP (B) & once per game; one step across a wall \textbf{owned by the opponent} \\
BLOCK (C) & once per game; a cell is impassable for exactly K plies \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}
\end{center}

Every action must leave a goal path for both pawns (pawns ignored; all blocks count, including
one that expires this ply: team Decision 9). A position whose mover has no legal action is a
draw; a 5-fold repetition is a draw.

\textbf{HOP change.} The proposal defined HOP as a once-per-game 2-cell jump over an opponent's wall
or pawn. Until commit \texttt{4e346a8} (2026-11-08) the engine used a 2-cell jump. Under that rule one
player always had a forced win: the pre-change exact solves (\texttt{person3\_pilot/solve\_N3\_K1\_M0.json},
\texttt{solve\_N4\_K1\_M0.json}) show that turning the old hop on flips the root value to the other
player within 2-5 plies in every A/C combination, even with no walls (N3: WIN in 3 becomes LOSS
in 2; N4: LOSS in 6 becomes WIN in 3). The team therefore changed HOP to the 1-cell step across
an opponent-owned wall above. Results made under the old rule are stale (\texttt{STALE.md}).

\textbf{Draws.} The proposal assumed binary outcomes. During engine work the team found positions
in which the mover has no legal action, although a goal path stays open: the mover's own pawn
can be boxed in after walls and blocks are used. N3 K1 M1 A1B1C1 alone has 48 such terminal
states (\texttt{final\_eval/solve\_N3\_K1\_M1.json}), and some exact roots are draws by endless play. Both
cases are draws (team rule R3, \texttt{docs/decisions.md} Decision 8), as is a 5-fold repetition.

\textbf{Truncation is not a draw.} The match runner stops a game at 200 plies and records
\texttt{truncated}, its own outcome. Every table here shows draw rate and truncation rate in separate
columns.

\section{2. Problem formulation and research questions}

\subsection{2.1 Formal model}

Modified Wallz is a finite, deterministic, two-player, zero-sum, turn-based game of perfect
information: an extensive-form game, equivalently a two-player zero-sum Markov game [1] with a
deterministic transition function. With the proposal's notation:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Players} N = \{0, 1\}; the player to move alternates every ply.
\item \textbf{Parameters:} board size N, block duration K, wall budget M, and the three rule switches
A (REPLACE), B (HOP), C (BLOCK). One setting of (A, B, C) is one cell of the 2\textasciicircum{}3 ablation.
\item \textbf{State} s = (pawn positions, the walls owned by each player, each player's latest wall,
walls left, hop used, block used, the block timer of each cell, player to move), plus the
position history used only for the 5-fold repetition rule.
\item \textbf{Actions} A\_i(s): the legal MOVE, WALL, REPLACE, HOP and BLOCK actions of Section 1. An
action is legal only if both pawns keep a path to their goal rows. The DQN indexes them in a
fixed space of 4(N-1)\textasciicircum{}2 + N\textasciicircum{}2 + 8 ids (33 on 3x3, 97 on 5x5), the size the proposal gives.
\item \textbf{Transitions} T(s, a) are deterministic; block timers count down by one per ply.
\item \textbf{Terminal states and payoffs:} reaching the goal row gives +1 to the mover and -1 to the
opponent; a mover with no legal action, or a 5-fold repetition, gives 0 to both (draw). The
200-ply cap is an experimental stop, recorded as \texttt{truncated}, not a game outcome.
\item \textbf{Solution concept:} the minimax value V*(s) in \{WIN, LOSS, DRAW\} for the player to move,
with the number of plies to the result under best play, computed by retrograde analysis [7].
For the DQN the comparable value is V* discounted by gamma = 0.99 per ply (+1 or -1 shrunk by
the distance to the result; DRAW = 0).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{2.2 Agents: what each decides and what each knows}

\begin{center}\scriptsize
\begin{longtable}{p{0.22\linewidth}p{0.22\linewidth}p{0.22\linewidth}p{0.22\linewidth}}
\toprule
Agent & Decides & Knows & Does not know \\
\midrule
Random & a uniform legal action & the legal actions & anything else \\
Greedy (Member 2) & the action with the best one-ply score & the rules; the distance score H = d\_opp - d\_self & the value of positions beyond one ply \\
Minimax (Member 1) & the best action by depth-limited alpha-beta search & the rules; a distance evaluation at the leaves & true values beyond its search depth; under a time limit, how deep it will get \\
Exact solver (Member 3) & a value-optimal action & the full game graph and V* (small boards only) & nothing: it is the ground truth \\
DQN (Member 4) & the legal action with the highest learned Q-value & the legal actions and the reward at the end of a game & V*, the transition model and any evaluation function: values are learned from self-play \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}
\end{center}

All agents see the full state (perfect information). The \textbf{unknown} quantity is the value of a
position: the search agents estimate it with a hand-made distance score, the DQN learns it from
interaction [2], and the exact solver computes it. This is the "known model vs learning unknown
values" setting of guideline direction C. The \textbf{resource limits} are guideline direction D: a
2.0 s per-move budget for Minimax and a fixed number of training steps for the DQN.

\subsection{2.3 Research questions and hypotheses}

The proposal (\texttt{report/project\_idea.pdf}, Sections 1 and 4.3) states three research questions
and four hypotheses:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{RQ1:} How do temporary and reusable spatial constraints (C and A) change what agents learn
and how they play?
\item \textbf{RQ2:} How does a one-time bypass resource (B) change long-term planning?
\item \textbf{RQ3:} How well do value-based learned policies approximate the exact game-theoretic values
as the board grows?
\item \textbf{H1 (branching):} A and C each raise the branching factor by at least 40\% and reduce the
depth Minimax reaches under a fixed time budget.
\item \textbf{H2 (sample efficiency):} DQN self-play with a heuristic warm-up reaches at least 75\% wins
against Greedy within 2x10\textasciicircum{}5 steps, versus at most 55\% for pure self-play.
\item \textbf{H3 (convergence):} the mean value error of the DQN on solvable boards (N <= 4) falls below
0.15.
\item \textbf{H4 (non-additive complexity):} all three mechanics together make games longer than the sum
of their single effects.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Success criteria} are the thresholds in H1-H3 and, for RQ3, how often the DQN picks a
value-optimal action and how far its values are from V*. Section 9 answers each question;
Section 12 lists where the work departs from the proposal and why.

\section{4. Agents}

\subsection{4.1 Greedy (Member 2)}

One-ply lookahead on \texttt{H = d\_opp - d\_self} (breadth-first distances), ties broken by action
category, own progress, then action index (\texttt{agents/greedy.py}). Deterministic unless seeded.

\textbf{Proposal tie order [proposal check].} The proposal breaks ties by maximum opponent distance,
then minimum own distance, then action index. This order is now available as
\texttt{greedy:order=proposal} (\texttt{tests/test\_greedy\_proposal\_order.py}); the default is unchanged, so
every earlier result still holds. Results are in Section 6.2.

\subsection{4.2 Minimax (Member 1)}

Negamax with fail-hard alpha-beta [8], a distance evaluation \texttt{U = tanh(0.2 (d\_opp - d\_self))},
move ordering and optional iterative deepening under a time limit (\texttt{agents/minimax.py}).
Pruning keeps root values exact: maximum difference 0.0 over 118 positions, and it cuts nodes
from 65,697 to 3,576 on 20 N5 positions at depth 2 (\texttt{final\_minimax/alphabeta\_equivalence.json}).
Move ordering saves 8.4\% of nodes at depth 2 (95\% bootstrap 3.5-14.2\%) on 50 N5 positions
(\texttt{final\_minimax/ordering.json}).

\textbf{Hop-aware scorer — new contribution, final stage [new].} \texttt{HopAwareEvaluator}
(\texttt{minimax:...,eval=hop}) measures a player's distance as the shortest path crossing at most one
opponent-owned wall while that player's hop is unused. It equals an engine-only search on 240
random positions (\texttt{tests/test\_minimax\_hop\_eval.py}), and the default scorer is unchanged: a full
rerun reproduced all 100 main games (\texttt{final\_minimax\_hop/default\_repro\_diff.json}).

\subsection{4.3 DQN (Member 4)}

Negamax Double DQN with a convolution stack (14 input planes, 64 channels, 256 hidden units),
masked arg-max, sparse +1 reward to the winner, gamma 0.99, self-play against the online
network, snapshots and Greedy [3, 4, 6] (\texttt{docs/report\_sections/member4\_dqn.md}). The action mask [5] equals
the engine's validator on all 5,952,580 ids checked (\texttt{final\_dqn/compat\_mask\_audit.json}).
\textbf{[new]} Retrained under the final rules: 3x3 (K1 M1) seeds 42-46 at 200,000 steps, 4x4 (K1 M0)
seed 42 at 500,000 steps (\texttt{checkpoints/final/*/manifest.json}).

\textbf{Released models [proposal check].} The released web models were retrained one after
another with \texttt{scripts/train\_dqn\_all.sh}: 3x3 seeds 42-46 (200k steps), 4x4 seed 42 (500k),
5x5 K3 M10 seed 42 (2,000,035 steps, the planned 2M) and 9x9 K3 M10 seed 42 (5,000,015 steps,
trained by a teammate, commit \texttt{cf5fe30}). Their evaluations are in \texttt{final\_dqn/overnight/}. The
retrained 3x3 models keep the value 95.8-96.7\% of the time over all non-terminal states, the
same range as the \texttt{checkpoints/final} models in Section 6.1.

\section{5. Exact solver and evaluation method}

\textbf{Solver.} \texttt{evaluation/state\_graph.py} builds the reachable position graph only through the
engine; \texttt{evaluation/retrograde.py} labels each node WIN, LOSS or DRAW for the side to move with
a rank (plies to the goal under best play) by backward attractor propagation, which handles
cycles. A capped graph reports UNKNOWN, never DRAW (\texttt{docs/report\_sections/member3\_eval.md}, 1-2).

\textbf{Validation [new].} \texttt{tests/test\_solver\_final\_rules.py}, 18 tests, all pass
(\texttt{final\_eval/test\_solver\_final\_rules.txt}); each edge is exactly one engine \texttt{apply\_action}; an
independent DFS + value-iteration reference agrees on every position; two broken engines make
4 and 3 tests fail (\texttt{final\_eval/mutation\_check\_solver\_tests.txt}); the legacy solver agrees on
all 16 N3 cells with 0 mismatches (\texttt{final\_eval/crosscheck\_N3\_K1\_M0.json}, \texttt{crosscheck\_N3\_K1\_M1.json}).

\textbf{Match method [new].} Greedy, Minimax without a time limit and DQN are deterministic, so a game
is fixed by its opening. Openings are random pawn moves; the opening length is the shortest with
enough distinct openings, and only the first seed of each distinct opening is played, in both
seatings. Rates and Wilson 95\% intervals count distinct games. Factorial effects use a paired
bootstrap over openings with Holm correction over the 7 effects.

\textbf{Metrics.} Win, draw, truncation and loss rates (Wilson 95\% intervals); mean game length in
plies; for the DQN on solved boards, the \textbf{value-optimal rate} (share of states where the chosen
action keeps V*), the \textbf{rank-optimal rate} (also the fastest win or slowest loss), sign agreement
of max Q with V*, and the \textbf{mean absolute error} between max Q and the discounted V*. These are
the proposal's metrics: win rate, truncation rate, mean length, optimal-action agreement and
mean value error.

\subsection{9.6 RQ2: what the one-time hop changes}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Game value:} with at most one wall per player the hop never changes a completed exact root
value.
On N3 M2 without A it turns a DRAW into a player 0 WIN in 7 (Section 6.1), so it matters once
walls are plentiful.
\item \textbf{Search agents:} B is the only effect on Minimax's win rate that survives Holm (-0.175).
It also lowers the draw rate by 0.122 and shortens games by 15.4 plies (Section 7).
\item \textbf{Planning with the hop:} a Minimax scorer that counts one hop across an opponent wall wins
11 points more against Greedy (Section 6.2). The default scorer therefore misjudges the value
of an unused hop.
\item \textbf{DQN:} B has no consistent effect on the DQN's value error (Section 9.5).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{9.7 RQ3: how well the DQN approximates the exact values as the board grows}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{3x3 (150,710 states):} 95.8-96.7\% value-optimal, value error 0.164-0.190 over five seeds.
Every model gets the game value against the exact solver in all 16 distinct openings
(Section 6.1).
\item \textbf{4x4 (M0):} 96.3\% value-optimal, value error 0.083. It still loses 3 of 8 drawn positions
against the exact solver.
\item \textbf{5x5 and 9x9:} no ground truth exists. Against Greedy the DQN wins 78\% on 5x5 (2M steps) but
50\% on 9x9 (5M steps), and 39\% against the proposal-order Greedy. Its relative strength falls as
the board grows, even with 2.5x more training steps.
\end{itemize}

\section{10. Discussion and conclusion}

The three mechanics act on different things.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{REPLACE and BLOCK make the game harder to search.} They raise branching by 136\% and 46\%
and take 2.1 and 0.6 plies off what Minimax reaches in 2.0 s. On 3x3, REPLACE turns every
M = 1 root into a draw.
\item \textbf{The hop decides results.} It is the only mechanic with a supported effect on match
outcomes. It is also the mechanic the original rule got wrong: the 2-cell jump made the game
a forced win.
\item \textbf{The mechanics interact.} Together they shorten games instead of lengthening them (H4).
\end{itemize}

On the learning side, a self-play DQN with a sparse reward and no model reaches 96\% value-optimal
play on boards with up to 150,710 states. Its value error falls below 0.15 on 4x4 but not on 3x3
within 200k steps. Which mechanics are on matters: REPLACE makes the values easier to learn and
BLOCK makes them harder. A Greedy warm-up does not help. On larger boards the DQN stays
clearly ahead of Greedy on 5x5 but is level with it on 9x9. On 9x9 its result also depends on
Greedy's tie order, so the 9x9 policy is the least robust.

Under a time budget the limit is computation, not the depth setting: depth 4 at 2.0 s is the
best Minimax setting on 5x5. Exact ground truth stops at about 10\textasciicircum{}6 states: N3 with up to two
walls per player, and N4 with one.

\section{11. Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{One seed} for 4x4, 5x5 and 9x9, and for each RQ1 cell and H2 arm. Only the 3x3 default
configuration has five seeds.
\item \textbf{No exact ground truth above 4x4.} The 5x5 and 9x9 results are match results against named
agents.
\item \textbf{Observation wall-owner alias:} the DQN input does not show who owns walls that are no longer
anyone's latest wall, yet HOP needs an opponent's wall; 14.6\% of owner-swapped 5x5 audit
positions change the legal set (\texttt{final\_dqn/encoding\_alias\_audit.json}). The trained 3x3 (M = 1)
and 4x4 (M = 0) cells are not affected; the 5x5 and 9x9 models are. Fixing this needs retraining
them, which the team did not do for compute reasons.
\item \textbf{Deterministic agents and distinct openings:} repeated openings replay games, so the
effective sample is the number of distinct games: 16 per seed on 3x3, 100 for the N5 main runs,
16 on N9. Older all-games rates (e.g. \texttt{final\_dqn/tables.md}) are superseded by
\texttt{final\_dqn/distinct\_reanalysis.json}. On 3x3 this also limits H2: the three arms play the same
games against Greedy.
\item \textbf{Timings under load:} N9 timed Minimax decisions took 359 ms in one run and 522 ms in a rerun
beside DQN training, with the same 20 games (\texttt{final\_minimax/vs\_greedy\_N9\_timed\_summary.json},
\texttt{final\_minimax/vs\_greedy\_N9\_timed\_r2\_summary.json}, \texttt{final\_minimax/n9\_r2\_load.json}). The 2.0 s
depth sweep was run on an idle machine but is still not reproducible move-for-move.
\item \textbf{The pilot's smaller effective n:} its 10 opening seeds held only 8 distinct openings
(\texttt{final\_eval/analysis.json} \texttt{opening\_counts\_N5}), so its intervals counted replicas.
\item \textbf{Capped cells:} the four C-on cells of N4 K1 M1 and seven of eight N4 K1 M2 cells were not
solved; no exact claim is made for them.
\item \textbf{Scope of the ablation:} it pairs Minimax depth 2 (untimed) with Greedy on one board (N5).
The DQN ablation is on 3x3 only.
\item \textbf{Provenance of the proposal-check results:} they were produced from a working tree at
commit \texttt{cf5fe30} with uncommitted changes (\texttt{dirty: true} in each file), under Python 3.14.3.
The 5x5 2M evaluation is from clean commit \texttt{6519e63}.
\end{itemize}
